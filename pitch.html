CRITSTORM

A push-your-luck incremental where the payout is not a number going up. It is molten gold, erupting into a falling-sand world that can burn it, boil it, or swallow it before you ever get to keep it.

Comps: Nodebuster for run structure and length, The Gnorp Apologue for a particle-sim economy you can watch. Gnorp renders wealth as matter. Critstorm makes matter mortal.

The pitch

Greed is the verb

Every unit of value in Critstorm is particles in the world. Currency never ticks up abstractly — it is erupted, it pools, it melts, it is lost or it is collected. If a number changes, something physical caused it.

That makes the failure state visible. There is no hidden bust roll. Riding a crit chain heats the world through a real thermal model: fire spreads, ice melts, your gold pool sits there at risk. You can watch the disaster coming and fight it with terrain.

The guardrail

Any feature that makes gold safe by default kills the whole differentiator. Automation is allowed to route wealth. It is never allowed to de-risk it for free.

That single rule decides what every upgrade in the game is permitted to do.

Moment to moment · about ten seconds

The loop

  1. Strike

    Click anywhere in the zone around the storm core. This rolls the crit chain — consecutive crits climb tiers 0 through 8.

  2. Erupt

    The payout erupts from the core toward your cursor as molten gold. Mass scales with the size of the payout; temperature scales with the tier. High tiers throw lava droplets and lightning with them.

  3. Cool & pool

    Molten gold flows, splashes off terrain, and cools into solid gold powder. On the way it ignites anything flammable and melts your own defenses.

  4. Collect

    Gold that reaches the collector converts to essence you can spend. Gold still lying in the world is the unbanked pot — earning nothing, at risk, waiting to be routed.

  5. Spend

    Essence buys crit upgrades, defensive materials you paint on with a brush, and structures that automate the routing.

The centerpiece

The surge — bank or ride

Clicking fills a heat meter. At full, a surge begins, and every strike from then on feeds a pot that is not an abstract number — it is molten gold visibly swelling inside the core, which glows brighter and bulges as it grows.

pot multiplier  M = 1.5n   for n crits landed this surge

Meanwhile the world heats from two directions. An ambient ramp climbs quadratically with every second of surge time — you cannot wait it out. And every crit injects a heat spike scaled by its tier, which is random. The same roll that pumps your pot is the roll that can end it. That randomness is load-bearing: a purely deterministic ramp would make "bank at 95% of critical" the one correct answer and there would be no gamble left.

Bank

The entire pot erupts at once as a mountain of gold. That is the spectacle — and it is physical, so it still has to survive the world and actually reach the collector. The surge ends and the world cools.

Overheat

Push past critical temperature and the surge detonates. The pot erupts as lava and fire instead of gold. You watch your winnings burn, and the world gold near the core melts with it.

The tuning target is that riding one more crit stops paying once the bust chance clears roughly one in three — which should land near six crits with no defenses and near ten with a good defensive layout. That gap is the entire reason to build defenses, so it has to be legible to the player.

Why the world matters

The world fights back

Materials

Molten gold ignites what it touches and cools into solid gold. Solid gold is a dense powder that sinks through water, melts back under heat, and dissolves in acid. Buried piles fuse into ingots so a fortune stays visually dense instead of flooding the screen.

Defense you paint

Stone baffles channel flows. Water quenches molten gold fast — which is both good and bad, because solid gold no longer flows to the collector. Ice is the strongest quench and melts under sustained heat. Walls are indestructible and cost accordingly.

Structures

The collector drains gold into essence at a fee. Magnets pull gold toward them. Auto-strikers fire on a timer. Sprinklers spray water. A lightning rod converts the storm's own lightning into free maximum-tier strikes.

Storm events

Gold rain is the jackpot — catch it before it lands somewhere hostile. Acid drizzle, lava fissures and lightning fronts are the threats. Both scale in cadence and severity the longer a storm runs, so the world itself is what pressures you to get out.

Fronts

The flats are open ground and mild weather. The bog buries oil pockets under plant growth — high fire risk, and much more gold rain to tempt you into it. The eye is a lava floor under constant lightning, and the win condition lives there.

The run

Three currencies, no menus

Gold→Essence→Storm cores

Every conversion between them is a physical action in the world, never a menu transaction. Gold is matter at risk. Essence is what survived the trip through the collector, and it resets each storm. Cores are permanent, and you earn them from the square root of what you banked — which means a storm twice as rich is not a storm twice as valuable.

A storm
Eight to thirty-five minutes. It ends when you bank out on your own terms and keep everything with a bonus on top — or when the world blows up and consumes everything you never routed.
Between storms
Spend cores in the workshop across four tracks: crit stats, collector economy, defensive kit, and access to new fronts. Then pick where the next storm lands.
The cost of greed
Banking out multiplies the cores you earn. Riding to a blow-up forfeits that multiplier, so about a third of a storm's permanent progress rides on knowing when to stop. Quitting while ahead has to feel smart, not cowardly.
The finale
In the eye, bank a single pot above the threshold at full multiplier and you trigger the CRITSTORM — a world-consuming golden storm, then the stats screen. Reachable in five to seven hours. A finite, designed ending.

  • Surge with the pot, the bank exit and the physical overheat bust
  • Collector, magnet, auto-striker, sprinkler, lightning rod
  • Stone and water brushes, painted per cell
  • Workshop across four tracks, plus the flats and the bog

  • The eye, and the CRITSTORM finale
  • Art direction, audio, and the juice on the bank and bust moments
  • Teaching a new player any of the above

The honest gap

The systems are in and the economy is tuned against a headless simulation harness rather than vibes. What is missing is the presentation layer and any explanation at all — a first-time player currently sees gorgeous physics and cannot tell you what they are supposed to want.

That is the next problem, and it is a known one.
